# Pilot scope and open questions

## Agreed scope

Start with one dispatcher and three to five drivers. The dispatcher adds an order to a chosen region. Eligible drivers everywhere can claim public orders immediately; drivers in the chosen region receive the initial notification. Customer self-booking remains outside this phase. Android shift behavior is described below.

Drivers must explicitly accept orders. Receiving an offer or leaving it open for five minutes never claims it or charges the balance. Hiding an offer is a local dismissal. Public popup and private reservation deadlines are fixed at 20 seconds; their different visibility rules are described below.

Dispatcher order creation requires a selected region, a valid customer phone and a manually entered positive price in TMT. Customer phone fields have a fixed +993 prefix and require exactly eight local digits. Addresses, map coordinates and notes are optional. After accepting, the driver calls the customer to agree on pickup details. Customer phones remain hidden from drivers before acceptance. Inherited orders without a quoted price do not debit the synthetic balance. Quoted orders reserve funds at acceptance and capture them at Start, as described below. Region-centre coordinates may be stored as a fallback and must not be presented as an exact customer pickup.

Public dispatch has no proximity ranking or regional acceptance priority. The selected region determines the initial popup audience. Optional pickup coordinates must not be described as an exact customer pickup when they are only a region-centre fallback.

Opening any driver region card shows the city-wide public claiming feed. Orders in the selected region appear first, followed by other regions; each row identifies its region. This does not change the card's regional order/driver counts or reserve work for nearby drivers. Private reservations, stopped and assigned orders stay out of the feed. The server rechecks every acceptance atomically, and a successful claim removes the order from all open feeds. Long feeds scroll inside the dialog while the driver home stays fixed.

The driver map fills the available viewport and shows local GPS plus valid pickup/destination points for the active trip. It has no district centre pins, district borders, region legend or route calculation. Pickup is emphasized before Start and destination after Start; missing or invalid points are omitted. The locate icon obtains a fresh fix when necessary and follows it until the driver pans. Stale local GPS on the map is muted with a visible last-position time; the dashboard shows passive status icons without explanatory text. Off-shift local GPS never uploads to the dispatcher; dispatcher monitoring behavior is unchanged.

Use fresh synthetic data, synthetic balances, no real payments, and TMT only. Test in mobile browsers first. Keep the supporting developer's current region editing environment intact; fixtures and test writes belong only in isolated environments. Fresh environments may load the saved canonical region defaults without overwriting existing boundaries.

Every order has a stable human-facing sequential `orderNumber` starting at 1. Dispatchers and drivers use this number when discussing an order (for example, “problem with order 9”); it is shared across cards, chat references, trip history, balance notes and filters. The UUID remains an internal API/socket identifier. Numbers are assigned by a database sequence, backfilled chronologically for legacy rows, and never reused after deletion.

Driver history hides customer phone numbers and internal UUIDs. The customer call action is available only on an active trip after the driver accepts it. Dispatcher order search has an exact order-number filter and independent exact-match customer-phone and driver-phone filters; both use canonical +993 normalization, including archived driver phone records.

Dispatchers can create approved driver accounts with a name, phone and password. Region selection is not part of account creation; the operating region is derived from GPS. New accounts start without a region, offline with a zero synthetic TMT balance; top-ups use the existing driver balance controls. Account approval is required; signing in on another phone never requires separate dispatcher device approval. Every active phone input has a fixed, noneditable +993 prefix and exactly eight local digits. Full +993 or 00993 numbers pasted in the UI normalize once without truncating invalid input. The API accepts +993 with eight digits, optionally separated by spaces or hyphens, and stores the canonical number; foreign numbers and invalid lengths are rejected. This validates number format, not ownership by SMS. Login now requires a valid Turkmen number too, so inherited non-Turkmen account identifiers must be corrected before rollout; no shared records are migrated automatically. A fresh default dispatcher seed uses +99360000000, while SEED_ADMIN_PHONE remains configurable.

Dispatchers can edit driver names and phone numbers, and reset passwords in a separate confirmation form. Name-only changes preserve sessions. A changed phone or password invalidates previous access tokens, refresh tokens and sockets, and takes the driver offline. Balances and active trips are preserved; the driver logs in with the new credentials to continue. Existing passwords are never displayed. New and replacement passwords require at least six characters and at most 72 UTF-8 bytes. Numeric passwords are allowed; passwords are never trimmed. Login preserves the supplied password without applying creation rules. SMS recovery and driver self-service credential changes are outside this scope.

The driver home shows every region in a fixed viewport grid. Each card shows its name and a public available orders:drivers score. The left number counts unassigned public orders available in that region; private reservations, stopped offers and assigned trips are excluded. The right number counts approved drivers on shift in that region, including those on a trip. A green card means its public available count is positive and clears when the last order is claimed or cancelled. A blue outline marks only the region containing this phone’s fresh local GPS while internet and dispatcher connectivity are healthy, even off shift. No fresh fix, no connection or a point outside every saved polygon means no blue outline; a persisted region from an earlier shift cannot create one. Blue outlines and green fills can appear together. The active trip shows its current stage, prominent price, customer call action and next lifecycle action directly above the region grid. Full route details and cancellation remain in the trip dialog. Incoming offers and order lists open in dialogs; long queues scroll internally while the page stays fixed. The home location row is read-only and uses browser GPS coordinates; manual region selection is removed from this screen. Region counts update through Socket.IO. Open order lists refresh on global region updates, reconnect and returning to the foreground; the home refresh control is removed. Active trips and balances restore from the backend after reload. Arrival selection is kept while an open list updates, and resets when that list is closed. The trip summary keeps its full route in the details dialog; the compact feed shows a two-line preview. Arrival selection survives live queue refreshes and reconnect while the queue stays mounted. Availability changes use brief visual feedback with reduced-motion support. Drivers obtain a fresh local GPS fix before going online. Off-shift GPS does not update shared driver tracking. One foreground convenience address lookup may submit a transient fresh point for display without changing the dispatcher map or region. Online sends the fresh fix and acquires exclusive shift ownership in one server transaction. Only the active owner uploads recurring GPS and can view available orders or claim them. Coordinates expire after 60 seconds; expiry takes the driver offline and stops new offers, while accepted-trip controls remain available. Restoring GPS requires explicitly going online again. Development uses browser GPS emulation; a separate in-app simulator remains future work.

Dispatcher broadcast history contains only admin-authored broadcasts. Each driver conversation merges its private messages with broadcasts sent since that user account was created, labeled as broadcasts. Driver history, unread counts, read cursors and native recovery share this cutoff. Private replies never enter the broadcast history or another driver conversation.

Driver and dispatcher chat keep the app header, composer and navigation inside the visible viewport. Message history scrolls independently. Enter sends, Shift+Enter adds a line, and text composition never submits a message. Empty or pending messages cannot be submitted; a failed send preserves its draft. Incoming messages follow the bottom only while the reader is already near it. Theme selection uses an icon dropdown with persisted system, light and dark preferences.

Drivers need a positive synthetic balance to go online or receive public work. A positive balance does not hide orders priced above it: those orders remain visible with acceptance disabled and a top-up explanation. Acceptance atomically requires enough available TMT for the quote and creates a durable hold. The driver dashboard total remains unchanged. An exact available quote is sufficient. Arrival retains the hold; only Start captures its snapshotted quote and records one PAYMENT. Completion never charges again. Dispatcher balance payloads show total, held and available funds, and every withdrawal or negative adjustment is limited to available funds. Private creation and reoffers check the selected driver's balance against the actual order quote, and acceptance rechecks it. Reservations do not charge or hold funds. Inherited unpriced orders retain their no-debit behavior and still require a positive balance for acceptance. Zero balance never removes accepted-trip controls or cancels a trip.

Public orders are immediately visible and claimable in every region by eligible online drivers. Only eligible drivers currently in the order’s region receive the 20-second incoming-order popup and corresponding native order alert. Other regions receive live queue/count updates without an incoming popup. This alert window never reserves public acceptance. Claiming or cancelling removes public availability and updates the driver grid for every viewer. Assigned unfinished trips stay in the driver’s active-trip feed and the dispatcher’s unfinished-work counts, separate from public available orders.

Private offers follow the [private-offer flow](private-offers.md): an eligible driver in any region explicitly confirms within a persisted 20-second deadline. The dispatcher chooses whether expiry stops delivery or simultaneously releases the order to every eligible region; the default is release. Public claims are global once released, and the first atomic claim wins. An early private decline dismisses delivery but does not expose the order before its original deadline. Expiry with fallback enabled opens all public queues without a second incoming-order popup. Reservations alone do not charge balances or expose customer phones.

Dispatcher chat uses a dedicated quiet 0.48-second chime, never the driver's order-alert music. Every signed-in admin tab outside the visible matching conversation receives the preview banner, including a hidden chat tab. Tabs for the same account share a three-second sound cooldown through browser coordination; different browsers/devices can each alert. Opening, dismissing or reading a message cancels its sound, including pending playback and sound in another tab. Leaving the dashboard shell also cancels that tab's current preview sound across the browser, even after local logout has cleared its account state. Sound cleanup does not mark messages read or change sessions. Dismissal does not mark it read. Replacing a preview silences its previous chime, and old read cursors cannot silence a newer unread message. Reconnect and unread recovery do not replay sounds. Unsupported audio, storage or Web Locks degrade to visual banners. See issue #120.

## Android shifts

Drivers remain available and receive orders while on shift with the app backgrounded, swiped away or the phone locked. Explicit Offline/logout ends availability. Force stop prevents Android delivery until the app is reopened, and the server lease expires when no fresh reports arrive. The native implementation uses a 60-second renewable lease and requires precise GPS and a working server connection before Online. Notification permission never blocks a shift. The app has no notification mute switch; OS notification settings remain the recovery control. See [Android testing](android.md) for current implementation, limits and required device verification. Browser-only execution does not provide this background guarantee.

Chat alerts are independent of GPS and shift, and reach every approved signed-in installation. Socket.IO handles foreground events; Android FCM is the fallback for missed or background delivery. The visible driver chat reconciles messages silently. Dashboard, Map and Profile receive message previews. Read state synchronizes across devices and removes the corresponding notifications. Each public or private attempt has its own server-owned event ID shared by sockets, inbox recovery, polling and FCM. Reoffers never reuse an old ID, and late invalidation of an old attempt cannot silence the new one. Stable event IDs prevent duplicate alerts, while logout, blocking and credential changes invalidate the old installation capability. Android Force stop and network or battery restrictions remain delivery limits.

Record answers here when settled, and use ADRs for resulting architectural choices. Track implementation gaps in GitHub Issues. [Architecture](architecture.md) distinguishes current behavior from this agreed scope.

## Production interface copy

Profile trip-history rows show status, recorded price, saved region and creation date. Expanding a row reveals available pickup/destination, a customer call action, notes, cancellation reason, lifecycle dates and order ID. Missing optional addresses and phones stay explicitly unspecified. A region-centre fallback never becomes an exact pickup. History pagination and balance records keep their existing behavior.

Driver profile uses one compact summary with identity, balance and vehicle rows, followed by existing trip/balance history. Operational labels use neutral balance wording. Test-environment banners and legacy navigation appear only in local Vite development and stay hidden in production builds. Driver dashboard keeps GPS beside a separate compact availability switch; each control retains a 44-pixel touch target. GPS status describes the local fix separately from internet and dispatcher connectivity. A fresh local fix remains green during connection loss; starting a shift and accepting work still require a successful server location report. A top banner distinguishes no internet from a reconnecting dispatcher connection. A running shift has 60 seconds to reconnect automatically on the same device; after expiry, Online must be tapped again and an assigned trip remains assigned. Chat and order alerts have no in-app mute control. Old stored mute values and compatibility requests cannot disable delivery. Browser permission and sound activation do not establish closed-app delivery. The online pulse requires both GPS readiness and a live dispatcher connection, with an amber switch during a connection outage. Synthetic data and payment restrictions remain unchanged; removing development copy does not enable real payments.

Driver availability blockers appear as compact inline text until GPS and balance requirements are met. Action errors and completion notices appear automatically above bottom navigation, with keyboard dismissal and a brief timeout. Errors inside open offer or trip dialogs remain inline. Background refreshes and returning to Orders from another tab do not repeat the same toast; a deliberate retry can show its failure again. An unsent dispatcher-chat draft survives tab switches and is cleared on logout.

## Driver location and native text interaction

The phone supplies GPS coordinates. Driver operating regions are assigned only by saved active dispatch polygons; outside every polygon, the previous region is cleared and the home shows `Вне районов` with the actual location. A nearest region center or Yandex administrative area must not substitute for a saved polygon. Being outside a polygon alone does not force the driver offline or add a new order-eligibility restriction.

Yandex reverse geocoding can show an optional street/building name. With fresh precise local GPS, internet and the visible home-screen location view, each signed-in installation may make one off-shift convenience request. Its transient coordinates never update shared tracking or the dispatcher map; a persisted installation marker prevents repetition on reload, reconnect or relaunch. An explicit successful Online action requests a fresh address again, with one exception to a convenience request's five-minute wait. Repeated Online actions cannot repeatedly bypass the shared driver limit. Thereafter the visible home-screen location view refresh automatically every five minutes, without a movement requirement; background views pause, and foreground/reconnect makes one catch-up request only when due. Manual refresh shares the same server limit. Dispatchers use region, coordinates and age without requesting or displaying address labels. The backend retains a shared daily cap with 20% reserve and provider backoff. Address caching is disabled unless permitted/configured. Missing configuration, quota exhaustion or failure shows region and coordinates; stale replies cannot replace newer points. Address requests never gate GPS, Online, claims or trip controls. See [geocoding](geocoding.md).

Driver labels, navigation and cards do not select text on long press. Inputs, textareas, editable content and explicit copyable content preserve text editing and selection. This treatment is scoped to driver routes, including portalled dialogs. Scrolling, keyboard focus and browser zoom remain available.

Dispatcher order lists use exclusive lifecycle filters: **Ожидают** shows unclaimed `NEW` orders; **В работе** shows assigned `ACCEPTED`, `WAITING` and `STARTED` orders. Completed and cancelled orders have their own lists. **Все** intentionally includes every status. The API retains legacy `active` (all unfinished orders) for existing clients.

Driver cancellation requires no reason and is allowed only in ACCEPTED, before arrival. It releases the hold without a PAYMENT or REFUND, frees the driver and creates persistent dispatcher review work. WAITING and STARTED reject driver cancellation. The dispatcher sees the order number and former driver in a recoverable banner; unresolved cancellations sort before terminal cancellations before pagination. The dispatcher can reoffer the same UUID and order number in its original saved region with a fresh 20-second attempt, or finally cancel it. Reoffer clears current assignment fields while preserving accepted quotes, former drivers and attributable audits. The former driver remains eligible. Terminal cancellations expose no resolution actions.

Dispatcher cancellation is allowed only in NEW, ACCEPTED or WAITING. It closes the unstarted order, releases held funds and frees the driver. Legacy trips already debited before this release refund their actual prior debit once on pre-start cancellation; starting them never creates a second hold or charge. STARTED orders reject ordinary cancellation with 409. Manual completion requires a reason, records service under the same driver and keeps the existing charge. Exceptional refunds/recovery are separate reviewed actions and must not be disguised as cancellation.

Диспетчер может отменить заказ только до начала поездки: NEW, ACCEPTED или WAITING. Отмена освобождает удержанные средства и водителя. Списание происходит только при начале поездки; ранее списанные до обновления средства возвращаются один раз. Для STARTED обычная отмена запрещена. Ручное завершение требует причину, сохраняет выполненную услугу и списание без возврата. Исключительный возврат или восстановление разбираются отдельно.

## Session recovery and revocation

Drivers must identify their installation at login, but only the account requires dispatcher approval. Several installations may stay signed in to the same approved account. A new login never revokes another phone's sessions or shift. Device identity stays internal; neither actor has a device approval or management screen. Only one device can own the driver's active shift: a second device cannot start another shift, upload GPS over the owner's location, end that shift or perform order actions. It can view account information and use chat. End the first shift before starting on another device; an assigned trip stays assigned to the driver. See [the ownership decision](adr/0004-separate-login-from-shift-ownership.md).

Device identity is bound to authenticated credentials. A missing identity is a 400 error. Logout ends that device's sessions and, if it owns the shift, its shift. Password or phone changes and account blocking still invalidate all devices. An obsolete logout cannot revoke a later login. Normal concurrent refreshes retain the bounded rotation grace while their session remains current. A device is an app installation or browser profile, not a verified physical phone; different driver accounts are not subject to a phone-wide lock.

When no shift is active, a signed-in installation of an approved account may recover the assigned trip without GPS or Online solely to finish it. Starting a shift makes its device the exclusive controller again. Logging out revokes every session from the same installation; an already-revoked logout token cannot end a subsequent login. Legacy credentials without a signed device identity require a fresh login.

Launch renders a connection-checking shell immediately, before session verification completes. A phone reporting no internet goes straight to a connection-retry screen without a verification request. Otherwise verification, including token refresh and its retry, has one eight-second deadline. Temporary network/server failures and that deadline preserve saved credentials. Retry is single-flight with visible pending feedback; internet recovery or returning to the foreground automatically retries a failed verification. Signed-out users can open the bundled login/registration screens offline, but cannot submit without internet. Protected workflows remain hidden until the session is verified again. A response from an older session cannot clear a replacement login or replay its mutation with the replacement driver's credentials.

## Account safety and off-shift location

Explicit Offline stops recurring location uploads even during an assigned trip; that trip remains assigned. A fresh local fix can stay green independently of internet or shift state. Each off-shift phone shows its own coordinates, not another phone's saved address. The dispatcher retains the last reported point with its timestamp and an Offline / unfinished-trip label when appropriate. Starting a shift validates the new fix and saved region in the same transaction that grants ownership; a competing phone is rejected without overwriting the working phone's position.

The dispatcher can sign out every session without a device list. This ends the shift and invalidates sessions and notification capabilities while preserving password, balance, approval and assigned trip. A lost phone also calls for a password reset. Temporary account blocking requires a reason and immediately ends access and the shift, even during a trip. It never cancels, refunds or reassigns that trip; the dispatcher resolves it separately. Unblocking restores eligibility to sign in, not an old session.

Delete account permanently removes access; there is no reactivation. History, balance, messages and links to old trips remain for records. Deletion is blocked until assigned trips are resolved. The released phone number may be used for a new application, which requires fresh dispatcher approval and starts with zero balance and no inherited history. It creates a new account, not a recovery of the deleted account.

The Android APK requests precise location and then optional notification permission after the first verified driver login. Denial or dismissal does not trigger another automatic prompt on reload or later login. The Online button provides manual location recovery through Android permission and location settings. The GPS and connection icons are passive status only. Android Settings controls notification permission; automatic retries and focus/reconnect checks only read permission. Permission onboarding never starts a shift or uploads an off-shift location. Visible driver location views in browsers obtain local GPS automatically without off-shift uploads. Dispatcher screens do not request driver location permissions.
